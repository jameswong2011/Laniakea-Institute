# Creation Myths

**Creation Myths** — the class of stories produced when stellar mentors answer a young species' first question — *how did it all begin?* — with a childlike answer: a compressed cosmology that becomes the cultural foundation from which a civilisation grows.

The thesis begins from a limit. The universal causality that set everything in motion is likely unknowable and unintelligible to consciousness at the human scale. Yet the question of origins is asked by every sentience at the dawn of its awareness — the first form of [[Curiosity and the Sacred Question|the sacred question]]. The [[Stellar Logoi|stellar systems]] and purveyors of evolutionary guidance that shepherd young worlds therefore answer the childlike question with a childlike answer, and the stories so created are the creation myths.

Each myth is a lossy, perspectival and load-bearing [[Compression and Decompression|compression]]. Lossy, because no telling at a young species' scale can carry the whole; perspectival, because each embeds real memetic insight about reality from the standpoint of its particular stellar faction; load-bearing, because the myth becomes the foundation from which the receiving civilisation grows outward, its imagery and assumptions quietly structuring everything later raised on top of it.

The category does double duty in the larger architecture. It explains why the world's cosmologies differ while rhyming — different mentors compressing one reality from different angles, the premise developed under [[Radical Syncretism|radical syncretism]] — and it grounds the method of reading scripture and channelled material as [[Trip Reports and Revelation|structured testimony]]: neither literal report nor mere invention, but a compression designed for the developmental moment of its audience. The account presupposes the sowing of young worlds by elder lineages, treated under [[Seeding and the First Ones]].
