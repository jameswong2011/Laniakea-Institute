# Ordered Closure

**Ordered Closure** — the four-stage protocol by which a computed world whose purpose has expired may lawfully end. The stages are recognition, disclosure, healing, and exit-or-renewal.

Even a world can be ended well or badly. From high above, shutting down a finished sub-reality looks efficient: it saves energy and returns isolated minds to a more coherent world. From inside, the very same shutdown can look like the apocalypse. So a lawful ending needs far more than a declaration that the experiment is over — it needs a procedure that gives the inhabitants standing as participants. A long-running online game can be closed by pulling the plug without warning, or by announcing the end, explaining why, helping players carry their characters elsewhere, and keeping a server running for those still living there. Whether a stuck world receives healing or protocol closure is settled by its position on the [[Shared Agency|shared-agency]] spectrum.

The four stages formalise that care. **Recognition** — the greater system acknowledges inhabitants as participants rather than surplus data. **Disclosure** — enough [[Gnosis and Its Timing|gnosis]] (liberating knowledge) to understand the world's limits and purpose, without destroying the capacity to choose. **Healing** — the dependencies, traumas, promises and false identities the enclosure created are repaired, so leaving is not merely flight from damage. **Exit or renewal** — inhabitants integrate into a fuller reality, consent to a transformed continuation, or keep a viable branch where life still contributes meaning.

Two dangers frame the sequence. First, as a world loses [[Simulacra and Fullness|fullness]] (fewer of its people remaining independent centres of experience), inhabitants increasingly meet projected figures, and the society becomes a hall of mirrors. Selective gnosis may then bend choice toward release, but it must never counterfeit consent. Freedom shaped by truthful understanding differs in kind from freedom engineered by hidden reward. Second, the opposite pathology: predatory closure *denied*. A [[The Demiurge|demiurgic]] system prolongs itself because its organiser evolves through the harvested suffering, attention or creativity of captive minds, and it promises selected inhabitants creator-status inside the engine so they will defend it. Its signature is scarcity — excessive simulation layers, declining fidelity, pleasure concentrated above, pain repeated below.

The ethics compress into a short test. Does the world generate value that can revise the greater order, or merely feed its controller? Are inhabitants full participants or decorative agents? Is variation metabolised or punished? Can the engine sustain the complexity it invites? Can the world end well? Does benefit circulate across scales, or accumulate upward? Where an enclosure hides the exit, ordered closure makes the exit an act of care. And there is an honoured alternative for those who serve: celestial realms are reward-bodies for those who gave up their own pleasure-function for the stability of the whole, the creator function harmonised into art-realms where an engine would have stranded it. That reading of heaven ties closure to [[Harvest|harvest]] and to [[Wrathful Repair|wrathful repair]].
