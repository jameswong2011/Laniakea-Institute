# Simulacra and Fullness

**Simulacra and Fullness** — the replacement for the crude split between "real" and "fake" worlds. Sub-realities are graded on five measures — energy, fidelity, fullness, depth and agency — and judged by whether their resolution matches the question they were built to answer.

Not every world carries the same weight of reality. In the [[The Fractal Multiverse|fractal multiverse]], some worlds are full emergent ecosystems whose inhabitants can alter the whole. Others are constructed *simulacra*: scaled-down copies with fixed parameters, reduced resolution, or characters generated only to answer one narrow question. "Real versus fake" cannot capture the difference. A dream can hold real fear; a game can produce real friendship; a lower-resolution world can still host genuine consciousness. So five measures replace the binary. **Energy** is the consciousness and computation spent to sustain the world. **Fidelity** is how closely it reproduces the relations it models. **Fullness** is how far its inhabitants are independent centres of experience rather than fixed functions. **Depth** is how many lower layers it can support without losing coherence. **Agency** is how far inhabitants can revise their world, refuse its purpose, or leave. Together these amount to an [[The Assay|assay]] of worlds, a graded sample with the yes-or-no verdict retired. (The vocabulary nods to Baudrillard's copies-without-originals, [[The Nietzsche–Baudrillard–Nāgārjuna Convergence]], re-valued here from deception to economics.)

A simulacrum saves [[The Pleroma|pleroma]] (the shared store of conscious substance) by narrowing its question. In a video game, fixed backgrounds and scripted non-player characters mean the engine need not decide everything at once, much as the [[The Lazy Render|lazy render]] computes the environment only where observation forces it. So low fullness is not automatically evil. A small model may answer a small question more clearly than a whole cosmos would; reduced fullness simply marks a subsystem solving a bounded sub-question for some greater process.

The danger begins when resolution falls below the complexity of the experience being studied. Richer admixtures (pain, love, embodiment, moral choice) demand finer substrata, and sometimes more nested layers. Every simulacrum has an appropriate "quantum size," set by its question. Stretch a low-capacity engine to host a high-complexity experience and you save energy only by injecting error into the answer — the exact shape of [[Sophia's Error]]. The opposite failure is a scarcity signature: excessive layering, sub-simulation stacked on sub-simulation, lets an intermediate entity borrow agency while marking a system cut off from source energy, a diagnostic of [[The Demiurge|demiurgic]] enclosure weighed under [[Ordered Closure|ordered closure]].
