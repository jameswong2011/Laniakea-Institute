# Simulacra and Fullness

**Simulacra and Fullness** — the replacement for the binary of "real" and "fake" worlds: sub-realities are graded on five measures — energy, fidelity, fullness, depth and agency — and judged by whether their resolution matches the question they were built to answer.

Not every world in the [[The Fractal Multiverse|fractal multiverse]] carries the same ontological density. Some are full emergent ecosystems whose inhabitants can alter the whole; others are constructed simulacra with fixed parameters, reduced resolution, or agents generated mainly to answer a narrow question. "Real versus fake" fails here: a dream can contain real fear, a game can produce real friendship, a lower-resolution world can host genuine consciousness. Five measures replace the binary: **energy**, the consciousness and computation committed to sustaining the world; **fidelity**, how closely it reproduces the relations it models; **fullness**, the degree to which inhabitants are independent centres of experience rather than fixed functions; **depth**, how many lower layers it can support without losing coherence; and **agency**, the extent to which inhabitants can revise their world, refuse its purpose, or leave it. (The vocabulary nods to Baudrillard's copies-without-originals — [[The Nietzsche–Baudrillard–Nāgārjuna Convergence]] — re-valued here from deception to economics.)

A simulacrum saves [[The Pleroma|pleroma]] by narrowing the question: fixed backgrounds and non-player functions reduce the decisions the deeper field must carry, much as the [[The Lazy Render|lazy render]] computes environment only where observation forces it. Lower fullness is therefore not automatically evil — it marks a subsystem solving a bounded sub-question for a greater dialectical process.

The danger begins when resolution falls below the complexity of the qualia being studied. Richer admixtures — pain, love, embodiment, moral choice — demand finer substrata and sometimes more nested layers; the appropriate "quantum size" of a simulacrum is set by its question, and stretching a low-capacity engine to host a high-complexity experience saves energy only by introducing error into the answer — the structure of [[Sophia's Error]]. Conversely, excessive layering is a scarcity signature: stacked sub-simulations let an intermediate entity borrow agency while marking a system cut off from source energy — a diagnostic of [[The Demiurge|demiurgic]] enclosure weighed in [[Ordered Closure|ordered closure]].
