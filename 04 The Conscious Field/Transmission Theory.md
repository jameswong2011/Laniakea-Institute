# Transmission Theory

**Transmission Theory** — the model of the brain–mind relation: the brain is an antenna, filter, cache and action-selector for a consciousness it receives, constrains and stores, but does not generate.

The claim follows from [[Consciousness as Primary]]. If consciousness is the universal capacity rather than a neural product, the organism's task is not to create awareness but to localise it — to tune the field to one body, one history, one survivable stream of relevance. The body is an interface: the brain acts as antenna, filter, cache and action-selector — a local memory and edge-storage node, not a generator.

The lineage is real. William James developed the transmission model in *Human Immortality*: the brain limiting and directing a wider consciousness rather than producing it from nothing. Aldous Huxley named the filter's character with the reducing valve: the nervous system constraining a cosmic consciousness to the survivable trickle a biological life requires.

The standard objection is neural injury: damage the brain and experience changes or vanishes, so surely the generator broke. But injury can impair the received signal, the available memory, or the capacity to express what is received, without proving that the brain created the entire field — a damaged receiver demonstrates local dependence, not the non-existence of the broadcast.

The model carries much of the surrounding architecture. It makes [[The Pineal Nexus|the pineal nexus]] intelligible as the receiver's coordinating hub; it reads dreaming as what the antenna does when the filter relaxes ([[Dreams as Creator-Space]]); it lets death prune the local interface while a deeper ledger persists ([[The Soul Ledger]]); and it reframes [[Uploading and Continuity|uploading]] as the copying of a cache rather than the transfer of a stream.
