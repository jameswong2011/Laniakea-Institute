# The Hard Problem of Consciousness

**The Hard Problem of Consciousness** — the question of why physical processing is accompanied by felt experience at all. The puzzle dissolves once the priority of matter over mind is inverted, at the price of a successor question left deliberately open.

Everything the brain does can be explained without ever explaining why it feels like anything. A complete inventory of neural firings can account for discrimination, report, attention, memory and behaviour, and you could read the entire wiring diagram of a machine that detects red and still learn nothing about what red looks like. Explaining the functions is hard but ordinary science; why performing them is accompanied by any inner experience is a different kind of question altogether. David Chalmers gave this its modern statement in "Facing Up to the Problem of Consciousness", separating the easy problems of function from this one hard residue.

The problem is hard only under an assumption there is no reason to accept: that matter comes first and mind must somehow be squeezed out of it. So framed, it was never really a puzzle. It was a category error — a materialism that put the effect before the cause, then marvelled that the cause would not follow. Invert the priority, let mind be the substance and matter its compressed expression ([[Consciousness as Primary]]), and [[Qualia|qualia]] no longer need manufacturing by neurons at all: they are the native actuality of relation between partitions of consciousness, received by the brain ([[Transmission Theory]]). There is no gap left to bridge, because feeling was never generated downstream of the physical in the first place.

Seen this way, the difficulty even points back at its source. A universe built entirely of distinctions is already saturated with the potential for feeling, since to store one pole of a difference is to store the other. The hard problem is stripped of its premise, which is not the same as being solved, and it stands as evidence against the metaphysics that produced it: the faith, as sorted from the method under [[Science and Scientism]].

Dissolution buys two things: no obligation to derive experience from non-experience, and an order in which felt actuality is basic, never a freak occurrence. It costs one thing: a successor question. How does a capacity with no viewpoint become a particular viewpoint — without conjuring consciousness from non-consciousness, and without declaring every possible partition already a full subject? That question is left open on purpose, filed among [[The Open Tensions]].
