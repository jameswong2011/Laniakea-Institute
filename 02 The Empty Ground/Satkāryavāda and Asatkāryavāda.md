# Satkāryavāda and Asatkāryavāda

**Satkāryavāda and Asatkāryavāda** — the old Indian dispute over where an effect comes from. *Satkāryavāda* says the effect already exists, hidden inside its cause. *Asatkāryavāda* says the effect is genuinely new, not there beforehand. The Madhyamaka lets both fight, then turns the whole quarrel against the ideas of cause and production themselves.

Take an everyday case: milk turning into yoghurt. The first camp says the yoghurt was latent in the milk all along — which is why milk sets into yoghurt and water never does. Production only *reveals* what was already present. The second camp says the yoghurt is a real novelty: the milk was not yoghurt, and now something new exists. Each side has caught something true. One honours the fact that causes constrain their effects; the other honours genuine emergence. Clay becoming a pot tells the same story from both sides.

Yet press either horn — either side of the dilemma — and it breaks. If the effect already exists in the cause, then "producing" it does nothing; the yoghurt is already there, so why the waiting and the culture? Production becomes pointless. But if the effect does *not* exist in the cause in any way, we are owed an account of how it arrives out of sheer absence, and none is forthcoming. Now notice the deeper snag. A thing counts as a "cause" only by actively producing an effect, and something is an "effect" only by being produced. Knock out a coherent notion of production and both cause and effect lose their footing together. The root verses press the point: how will what is empty arise, and how will what is empty cease? What is empty is unarisen and unceasing. This sharpens [[The Catuṣkoṭi|the four-cornered negation]] and confirms the absence of *svabhāva* — independent, self-contained being ([[Śūnyatā (Emptiness)|emptiness]]).

So what is actually real? Not the perceived object, which is only partly real, and only as an *effect*. Full reality belongs to the conscious experience itself ([[Consciousness as Primary]]) and to the compressed [[The Seed Phrase|seed phrase]] from which a world is unpacked. Everyday reality has no inherent existence, and anything that *did* carry a fixed, self-contained nature could neither begin nor end ([[Dependent Origination|dependent origination]]).

The stakes are not merely academic; they are *soteriological* — bearing on liberation. To insist that arising and ceasing are ultimately real is itself a symptom of the karmic bond that ties us to *samsara*, the round of birth and death ([[Karma as Contextual Law]]). Step outside the very distinction between bondage and release, and what remains is ultimate reality — reached only by the middle way ([[The Two Truths|the two truths]]).
