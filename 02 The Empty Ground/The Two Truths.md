# The Two Truths

**The Two Truths** — the teaching that reality can be described truthfully on two levels at once. *Conventional truth* describes the stable patterns of everyday life. *Ultimate truth* is what careful analysis finds beneath them: no fixed, independent substance at all. Held together, the two levels stop [[Śūnyatā (Emptiness)|emptiness]] from collapsing into flat denial.

Conventional truth is not a polite lie. Fire burns, promises bind, institutions hand out power, care changes how a child develops, and choices bear consequences. Ultimate analysis overturns none of this. It finds only that no isolated, self-standing substance sits underneath the patterns: their reality is a matter of dependent action, not permanent ownership, the point developed as [[Dependent Origination|dependent origination]]. The two levels answer different questions, and holding both at once, letting neither cancel the other, is a basic discipline of clear thinking. A map is the everyday parallel: genuinely useful and genuinely true for travel, yet no one confuses the printed contour-line for the actual hill.

The governing image is the mirage. Conventional reality is *mirage-like*, which does not mean meaningless. A mirage is a real appearance, with real causes, that can genuinely affect where you steer, and it becomes a *falsehood* only if you take it for water existing the way it looks. In the same way, pain is not cancelled by discovering that the self in pain has no intrinsic existence ([[Anātman (No-Self)|no-self]]). On the contrary: emptiness is exactly what explains how pain can arise, spread, shift and end. A world of fixed essences could never change. A dependently arisen world can. That is why the analysis *increases* the weight of consequence and dissolves none of it.

The same structure protects value. Good and bad are like the two banks of a river: each takes its shape from its relation to the other, while the river of action runs between them. None of this makes morality arbitrary. Moral meaning depends on a whole value-bearing ecology, and on no isolated essence — which is how karmic consequence ([[Karma as Contextual Law]]) can run on empty ground. The two truths are also the main defence against the nihilist misreading of emptiness, the incomplete demolition diagnosed in [[The Nietzsche–Baudrillard–Nāgārjuna Convergence|the convergence]]. And they return as a living method in the paired movements of building and dissolving, [[Svātantrika and Prāsaṅgika]].
