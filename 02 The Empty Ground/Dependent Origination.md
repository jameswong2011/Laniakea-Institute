# Dependent Origination

**Dependent Origination** — the principle that nothing arises from its own side: every phenomenon comes into being in dependence on conditions, carries no identity within itself, and ceases when its conditions withdraw. The doctrine is not merely allied to emptiness but identical with it.

The identity is stated as the pivotal move of the whole analysis: whatever is dependently arisen is thereby [[Śūnyatā (Emptiness)|empty]] of *svabhāva*, intrinsic existence. A flame depends on fuel, oxygen, chemistry, perception and the concept by which it is recognised; withdraw the conditions and the apparently self-contained thing dissolves. The conclusion is ontological rather than merely epistemic: there is no layer of self-standing atoms beneath the web of relations — the web is the reality. Relation does not happen *between* finished things; apparently finished things precipitate from relation, a reversal developed as [[Process Ontology|process ontology]].

The engine of the argument is Nāgārjuna's critique of causation, compressed into a dilemma: if the effect already exists latent in its cause, its production is superfluous; if it does not, no adequate account of production is forthcoming. Anything genuinely non-empty would possess a fixed, determinate nature incompatible with arising and ceasing — so conventional reality, forever arising and dissolving, is devoid of intrinsic existence, and even origination itself is empty. [[The Two Truths|The two truths]] keep this from cancelling consequence: dependence is precisely what lets fire burn and choices bear fruit.

Classical Buddhism unfolds the principle as the twelve-linked chain of conditions running from ignorance to ageing-and-death. Here the chain contracts to three links keyed to the larger cosmology: ignorance of the dharma-collaboration structures in their fullness precipitates formation into physical or astral shape, which then demands the processing of information relative to material reality and so gives rise to mundane consciousness. Descent into a rendered world is thus cognitive before it is physical — a forgetting of the generative [[The Seed Phrase|seed phrase]] from which the world unpacks. Downstream, the same logic grounds [[Anātman (No-Self)|no-self]], since agent and act arise as one dependent event.
