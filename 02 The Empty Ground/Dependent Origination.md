# Dependent Origination

**Dependent Origination** — the principle that nothing arises from its own side. Everything comes into being by leaning on conditions, holds no identity within itself, and passes away when those conditions withdraw. This is not merely allied to emptiness; it *is* emptiness, viewed from the causal angle.

Here is the plain version: nothing stands alone. Take a flame. It depends on fuel, oxygen, a certain temperature, the chemistry of combustion, and even the eye and the word that pick it out as "a flame". Withdraw any one condition and the seemingly self-contained thing is gone. This is the pivotal move of the analysis. Whatever is dependently arisen is, for that very reason, [[Śūnyatā (Emptiness)|empty]] of *svabhāva* — independent, self-contained existence, the property of being what you are all on your own. The point is about what exists, not merely about what we can know. There is no layer of self-standing atoms beneath the web of relations. The web is the reality. Relation does not happen *between* finished things; apparently finished things precipitate out of relation — a reversal carried forward as [[Process Ontology|process ontology]]. Modern physics reaches the same verdict from the far end: its particles turn out to be knots of relation with no private core ([[Ontic Structural Realism|structure without substance]]).

The engine is Nāgārjuna's analysis of cause and effect, run as a dilemma. Consider a seed becoming a sprout. Either the sprout already exists inside it — so growing it is pointless — or it does not, and we owe an account of how it comes from nothing. Both horns fail. Anything with a truly fixed, self-contained nature could neither begin nor end, because a fixed nature has no room to change. So ordinary reality, forever arising and dissolving, cannot be running on such natures. Even origination itself is empty. This dilemma is set out in full under [[Satkāryavāda and Asatkāryavāda|the pre-existence dispute]], and the instrument that closes every escape route is [[The Catuṣkoṭi|the four-cornered negation]]. None of this cancels consequence: [[The Two Truths|the two truths]] keep dependence as exactly what lets fire burn and choices bear fruit.

Classical Buddhism unfolds the principle as a chain of twelve conditions, running from ignorance to ageing-and-death. Here the chain contracts to three links keyed to the wider cosmology. Ignorance of the collaboration-structures of reality precipitates formation into a physical or astral shape. That shape must then process information relative to the material world, and mundane consciousness arises. Descent into a rendered world is therefore cognitive before it is physical — a forgetting of the generative [[The Seed Phrase|seed phrase]] from which the world unpacks. Downstream, the same logic grounds [[Anātman (No-Self)|no-self]], since actor and act arise as one dependent event.
