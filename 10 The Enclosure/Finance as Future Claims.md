# Finance as Future Claims

**Finance as Future Claims** — the structural account of money and credit: money as compressed social trust, credit as a promise carried from future production into present action, and financial corruption as the point at which this encoding is made illegible to those bound by it.

Money is a promise you can hold. A banknote is [[Compression and Decompression|compressed]] social trust, a web of "I will honour this" folded into one token, and credit stretches that promise across time, carrying tomorrow's production into today's action — kin to the [[Promises and the Hierarchy of Freedom|promises]] that let strangers collaborate at all. So finance can coordinate action across decades with great power, and its corruption bites just as deep. Corruption starts when the encoding grows so opaque that the person making the promise can no longer see who holds it, or how its value is quietly changing.

Opacity then hardens into a moat. Institutions charge a toll for guiding people through rules they helped make confusing. Beneath the toll sits measurement politics, the quiet rewriting of the numbers everyone trusts. Inflation shifts value through changes that never show up in a nominal balance. Tax on nominal gains can treat currency debasement as profit. Official totals can omit precarious work, or adopt adjustments under which real decline is hard to name. In the notation of [[Measurement as Transformation]], the published figure Aa becomes the official reality, while the omitted remainder Ab is borne privately.

There is a deeper reading. Finance works as a control grid on the material plane, the lower-dimensional cousin of [[Karma as Contextual Law|karmic debt]]. Where the higher courts settle across many lives, a financial system settles within one, binding attention to obligations never freely chosen and so narrowing a person's [[Free Will and Decision-Space|room to choose]]. Its instrument is architecture, not open force: no command is ever issued; you are simply enclosed by rules too tangled to leave.

The analysis reaches the international layer; it describes structure and predicts no events. Whoever issues the world's reserve currency earns seigniorage (profit from minting money others must hold) and can project power through settlement networks, sanctions and access to liquidity. A multipolar world weakens the odds that any single national currency stays the unquestioned global measure. A shift toward hard collateral, or several interoperable settlement systems, would be a monetary phase change: old claims repriced, exported costs returned to their issuer.

The test is standard-agnostic. No monetary design delivers justice by itself. Every monetary [[The Logos|Logos]], every ordering system for value, must make its extraction legible. A currency whose stability depends on permanent information asymmetry is, within [[The Farmed Planet]], a farm instrument. The unrestricted accumulation it enables ends in [[Techno-Feudalism]].
