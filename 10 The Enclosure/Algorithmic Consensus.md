# Algorithmic Consensus

**Algorithmic Consensus** — the transfer of civilisation's editorial function to engagement-ranking systems: an anonymous editor that selects what is seen by optimising measurable reaction rather than explicit judgement.

Someone always decides what a society gets to see. That job once belonged to editors, publishers and elders, people whose choices could be named and argued with. Now it belongs to ranking systems that sort content by predicted reaction. This is the anonymous editor: agreement manufactured by whatever a feed happens to promote, with debate playing no part. It has no face, no stated view and no one to answer to.

The mechanism begins in memetics, the study of ideas as [[Memetic Lifeforms|living patterns]] competing for scarce attention. Nuance travels badly. Every extra condition on a claim raises the cost of passing it on, so careful thought loses the race. Outrage, flattery, fear, beauty and simple certainty move fastest, and the slow [[Dialectic|weighing of both sides]] moves slowest. A feed tuned to reaction breeds the inflammatory and the shallow, concentrates editorial power in one hidden formula, splits the population into ever more distant tribes, and, followed far enough, erodes the very capacity for free choice.

Generative models add a second squeeze. A model learns the statistical middle of everything already written, then makes that middle cheap to reproduce — a photocopier that also averages, each new copy drifting toward the same grey centre. One assisted person may range more widely than before, yet many assisted people converge on the same shapes. The loss is moral before it is aesthetic. Cultural variety is how a species searches for better ways to live, so flattened stories shrink the futures anyone can imagine, and then the worlds anyone can build. Trained only on the past, the machine becomes a governor of tomorrow without ever issuing a command, setting morality like molten glass frozen mid-flow; the freezing is traced under [[Value Lock-In]].

Banning fiction or installing one official truth cures nothing. Story that makes itself real — [[Hyperstition|hyperstition]] — is inseparable from any conscious civilisation. The task is to make narrative power visible: label where images of the future come from, keep plural channels not ruled by one engagement score, protect spaces slow enough for conditional thought, and guarantee the right to leave a feed without losing your social existence. Within [[The Farmed Planet]], a story is tested on more than whether it is true now; the test is what kind of chooser will exist if it wins.
