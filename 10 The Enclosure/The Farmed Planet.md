# The Farmed Planet

**The Farmed Planet** — the central civilisational diagnosis: reality, in its corrupted local form, is a spiritual farm. A farm is any system in which the growth of one life-form is organised primarily for the benefit of another; a civilisation becomes a spiritual farm when its institutions cultivate attention, labour, desire, fear, health and belief mainly as extractable outputs.

Start with an ordinary farm. The animals are fed and sheltered, yet their lives are organised for someone else's yield. A civilisation's institutions may house, entertain and protect people while running on them for what can be extracted: attention, labour, fear, desire, belief. None of the comforts change the decisive fact — the inhabitants' purposes come second to the harvester's. What is drawn off is spiritual energy, the [[Harvest|harvest]] the farm is kept for.

No secret committee is required. Incentives alone can push separate institutions toward one outcome: a population good at producing and consuming, yet steadily less able to say why it does either. The enclosure has a cast — the herded, the herders, and the few who see the pattern and can move it.

The modern farm can be read institution by institution. Each pairs an honest function with a farming inversion — the same body turned to extraction. Education should wake curiosity and train reasoning; it inverts into credentialed obedience ([[Education and the Incapacitation of Learning]]). Religion should bind suffering to purpose; it inverts into ready-made certainty ([[Curiosity and the Sacred Question]]). Finance should share risk across time; it inverts into opacity and claims on future labour ([[Finance as Future Claims]]). Media should widen our shared picture of reality; it inverts into manufactured consensus and shattered attention ([[Algorithmic Consensus]]). Health and food drift from nourishment toward engineered appetite; digital infrastructure, from shared memory toward surveillance and choosing what counts as real.

This is a relation between institutions, not a verdict on the people inside them. A teacher can wake minds where the system rewards standardisation; a doctor can heal inside a payment structure built around treatment. No individual malice is needed.

So every institution meets one question: does it serve human flourishing, or farm human energy? The test is agency — whether people can understand and change the systems that hold them ([[Agency as the Measure]]). A comfortable enclosure is still an enclosure once every exit has been made materially, mentally or spiritually unavailable. Yet the farm is only one of four things a rendered world can be at once ([[Womb, School, Farm, Prison]]), which is why the diagnosis stops short of total condemnation.

To see the enclosure clearly and keep your moral compass is [[Lucid Despair]]. Where an institution's incentives have fused with the harm it repeats, reform only hands the old body new words. The way out is demolition in service of regrowth — new roots from the rubble.
