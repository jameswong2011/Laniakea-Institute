# The Farmed Planet

**The Farmed Planet** — the central civilisational diagnosis: reality, in its corrupted local form, is a spiritual farm. A farm is any system in which the growth of one life-form is organised primarily for the benefit of another; a civilisation becomes a spiritual farm when its institutions cultivate attention, labour, desire, fear, health and belief mainly as extractable outputs.

The definition's scope is precise. A farm may keep its inhabitants alive, entertain them, breed them and protect them from some dangers; none of this removes the decisive fact that the inhabitants' purposes are subordinate to the harvester's purpose. Nor does the diagnosis require a central plan: incentives alone can make many institutions converge on the same result — a population capable of production and consumption but progressively less capable of defining the reason for either. The enclosure has its cast: cattle, herders, and the few who see the plot and hold agency to influence it.

The modern enclosure can be tabulated institution by institution, each legitimate function paired with its farming inversion. Education exists to awaken curiosity and train reasoning, and inverts into credentialed obedience and the disabling of synthesis ([[Education and the Incapacitation of Learning]]). Religion exists to connect suffering to purpose, and inverts into ready-made certainty ([[Curiosity and the Sacred Question]]). Finance exists to account for promises and share risk across time, and inverts into opacity, rent and claims on future labour ([[Finance as Future Claims]]). Media exists to enlarge the [[The Assay|common assay]] and host public dialogue, and inverts into manufactured consensus and fragmented attention ([[Algorithmic Consensus]]). Health and food systems drift from repair and nourishment toward symptom management and engineered appetite; digital infrastructure drifts from shared memory toward surveillance and reality-selection.

The farm is an emergent relation between institutions, not a moral description of the people inside them. A teacher may awaken minds within a system that rewards standardisation; a physician may heal within a reimbursement architecture built around treatment; a religious community may bear grief while its doctrine discourages questions. No per-worker malice is required.

Every institution can therefore be evaluated by one question: does it serve human flourishing, or farm human energy? The audit criterion is agency — whether people can understand and alter the systems that sustain them ([[Agency as the Measure]]). A comfortable enclosure remains an enclosure if every exit has become materially, conceptually or spiritually unavailable. In the wider cosmology the farm is one of four functions a rendered world can serve at once ([[Womb, School, Farm, Prison]]), which is why the diagnosis stops short of total condemnation.

Recognition of the enclosure with the moral compass still active is [[Lucid Despair]]. And where institutional incentives have become inseparable from the harms they reproduce, reform only gives the old body new language: the way out is demolition in service of regrowth, so that new roots can spring from the rubble.
