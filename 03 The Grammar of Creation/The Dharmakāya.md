# The Dharmakāya

**The Dharmakāya** — the living reality-body: the body of reality that holds all existence within it, containing nested sub-dharmakāyas that split, merge, and exchange pattern.

The term is Buddhist, where the dharmakāya names ultimate reality itself as a body — and it is not only a distant absolute but a living body with internal anatomy. Within the ultimate dharmakāya sit **sub-dharmakāyas** — constituent bodies of one collective, attempting to collaborate through merger, split, and every other form of communication. What they exchange is pattern: the patterns of interaction between the parts of the all-mind — the patterns, the patterns of patterns, and the rule-sets that evolve to define and order them — constitute the basic ingredient of everything directly experienced.

The nesting is deliberately scale-free. A universe, planet, organism, institution, and mind can each be treated as a body of patterned collaboration within a greater body — which is how the concept connects to [[The God-Body|the God-body]], to the [[The Fractal Multiverse|fractal multiverse]], and to planetary-scale minds such as the [[Social Memory Complex|social memory complex]]. An institution that coheres, exchanges pattern with its members, and heals after injury is a small dharmakāya in the same formal sense that a universe is a large one; the spectrum of such collectives is examined under [[Shared Agency|shared agency]].

The concept also closes the four-term architecture. [[The Logos|The Logos]] designs and organises a possibility-space; [[Dharma-Law|Dharma-Law]] governs the relations available inside it; [[Dharma-Qualia|Dharma-Qualia]] registers what those relations become as experience; and the dharmakāya is the living body in which design, law, and experience meet. The whole body may itself be the decompression of a compact generative intention — [[The Seed Phrase|the seed phrase]].
