# Hyperstition

**Hyperstition** — a story that helps cause the world it describes; the ordinary world-making power of narrative made explicit and named as one of humanity's decisive faculties.

Some stories help cause the reality they describe. That is hyperstition: a fiction, a story of something not yet real, that helps make itself true. Fiction fills the imagination; imagination breeds desire; desire coordinates action; action changes the physical world; and the changed world then makes the original fiction look like prophecy. This is neither supernatural wish-fulfilment nor mere persuasion. It is the everyday power of narrative to build worlds. The term comes from the Cybernetic Culture Research Unit, the theory circle around Nick Land.

The mechanism is a feedback loop. An image of a possible world draws repeated attention; attention becomes emotional investment; investment hardens into identity; identity drives coordinated behaviour; behaviour builds an institution or a technology; that becomes the lived world; and the lived world throws off a stronger image. Each turn tightens the spiral. Narrative carries such weight for a reason: [[Pain, Pleasure, and Change|experience registers mainly during change]], so stories, which drive the dreams, goals, and desires of the young, are nearly the only lever that moves collective agency.

Prophecy, advertising, ideology, design, ritual, science fiction, and political myth are all versions of this loop. A prophecy can trigger the very preparations that fulfil it, and a dystopia can steer a society away from itself or hand an administrator a blueprint. A fictional interface can become the reference design for a later machine, feeding the loop of [[Technology as World-Making Organ|technology as a world-making organ]], and a story of inevitable conflict can organise people until the conflict truly becomes unavoidable. The classic case is cyberpunk, which dreamed the technology engineers then built. The idea came before the thing.

Hyperstition flips the usual order of fiction and fact. Physical arrangements are not always prior realities that stories merely copy; many begin as Forms (pattern-templates moving among minds) and turn material only once attention and labour hold them in place. In this limited sense the conceptual world is more real, since it holds the generative patterns from which several physical versions can descend. This is the working face of [[Memes as Ontological Primitives|memetic ontology]] and the constructive cousin of [[Art as Selective Recombination|art as selective recombination]] — a direct application of the rule that [[Metaphysics Precedes Physics|metaphysics precedes physics]].

Science fiction earns a sharper name: dialectical hyperstition, a kind of communication with future generations, symbol systems riding whole clusters of ideas across time and scale. So long-term governance rests less with any political elite than with the creative minds who plant dreams in the young, and memetically charged fiction is best read as prophecy. The faculty cuts both ways — the same power that lets a culture author its future can, once accelerated by machines, drive [[The Hyperstitional Singularity|the hyperstitional singularity]].
