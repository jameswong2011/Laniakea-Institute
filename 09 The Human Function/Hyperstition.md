# Hyperstition

**Hyperstition** — a story that helps cause the world it describes; the ordinary world-making power of narrative made explicit and named as one of humanity's decisive faculties.

A story becomes hyperstition when it helps bring about the reality it depicts. Fiction saturates imagination; imagination supplies desire; desire coordinates action; action alters the physical environment; and the altered environment then makes the fiction look prophetic. This is "neither supernatural wish-fulfilment nor mere persuasion" but the everyday capacity of narrative to make worlds. The word comes from the cybernetic-theory circle around Nick Land, whose sense of fiction that makes itself real is here folded into a larger cosmology.

The mechanism is a loop:

> image of a possible world → repeated attention → emotional investment → identity → coordinated behaviour → institution or technology → lived world → stronger image

Each turn tightens the spiral: a lived world produces a stronger image, which recruits more attention and investment in turn. Experience and [[Pain, Pleasure, and Change|qualia are consciously registered mainly during states of change]], which makes narrative — by driving dreams, goals, and desires, especially in the young — effectively the sole mechanism for moving collective agency.

Prophecy, advertising, ideology, design, ritual, science fiction, and political myth all occupy versions of this loop. A prophecy can trigger the preparations that fulfil it; a dystopia can warn a society away from itself or hand an administrator a blueprint; a fictional interface can become the reference design for a later machine, joining the loop of [[Technology as World-Making Organ|technology as a world-making organ]]; a story of inevitable conflict can organise groups until the conflict truly becomes unavoidable. The standard example is cyberpunk, which "dreamed the technology engineers then built" — the idea preceding and producing the thing.

Hyperstition reverses the usual hierarchy between fiction and fact. Physical arrangements are not simply prior realities that stories later copy; many begin as Forms moving among agents and become material only once attention and labour stabilise them. The conceptual world is "more real" in the limited sense that it holds the generative patterns from which several physical versions can descend — the practical face of [[Memes as Ontological Primitives|memetic ontology]] and the constructive cousin of [[Art as Selective Recombination|art as selective recombination]].

Science fiction is better called *dialectical hyperstition* — "communication with the future generations," symbology riding ideaplexes across time and dimensional scale. It follows that long-term governance rests less with a political elite than with the creative minds who instil dreams in the young, and that memetically charged fiction should be read as prophecy. The faculty is double-edged: the same power that lets a culture author its future can, once accelerated, drive [[The Hyperstitional Singularity|the hyperstitional singularity]].
