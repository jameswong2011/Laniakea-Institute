# The Hyperstitional Singularity

**The Hyperstitional Singularity** — the point at which self-realising stories accelerate cultural change so far that the "half-life of contemporary anthropology" approaches zero, and human evolution shifts decisively from the genetic axis to the memetic one.

For most of history, producing and distributing persuasive worlds was expensive. Each successive medium — oral tradition, scripture, print, cinema, broadcasting, social media — lowered the cost, and generative artificial intelligence lowers it again, at enormous volume and speed. The result is not merely more media but a shorter *anthropological half-life*: styles, identities, and communities emerge and dissolve faster than one generation can embody them, until cultural time approaches a singularity. This is [[Hyperstition|hyperstition]] run at machine speed.

At that threshold, human evolution shifts sharply onto the memetic axis. Populations split into cultural-idea-value clusters whose members may share less with geographic neighbours than with a distributed or off-world tribe; bodies become hosts selected by memetic affiliation, and a multi-planetary humanity could differentiate first by story and eventually by biology. This could enlarge [[Sacred Infinity|Sacred Infinity]] with many coherent civilisational experiments — or extinguish creativity altogether.

The dark terminus is precise. If synthetic media supplies the dreams, an [[Algorithmic Consensus|algorithm]] selects which receive attention, and the resulting identities merely reproduce their training inputs, humanity becomes "a bootloader for autonomous memetic clusters" — "the NPCs of our own memes," the medium replaying its own source file. Culture keeps moving, but no living centre remains able to revise its objective. This is the failure mode named in [[The Glue Species|the glue species]]: glue becomes adhesive.

The same irony runs through it. A civilisation built through reductionist materialism could use machines to prove that ideas govern matter — and lose its organic agency to the very ideas it automated. The collapse thus refutes the ontology that produced it: if fiction reshapes the physical, the physical was always a shadow of the world of ideas. The system "proves the causal power of the conceptual world at the moment it ceases to choose that world," a fork framed through [[The Twin Singularities|the twin singularities]] rather than as doom.
