# The Hyperstitional Singularity

**The Hyperstitional Singularity** — the point at which self-realising stories accelerate cultural change so far that the "half-life of contemporary anthropology" approaches zero, and human evolution shifts decisively from the genetic axis to the memetic one.

Culture is speeding up toward a limit. For most of history, building and spreading a persuasive world was expensive. Each new medium made it cheaper (oral tradition, then scripture, print, cinema, broadcasting, social media), and generative artificial intelligence makes it cheaper again, at vast volume and speed. The result is a shorter anthropological half-life — the time it takes for half of a culture's styles, identities, and communities to be replaced. These now appear and dissolve faster than a single generation can live them out, and cultural time contracts toward a singularity, a point of change too fast to track. This is [[Hyperstition|hyperstition]], fiction that makes itself real, run at machine speed.

At that threshold, human evolution swings hard onto the memetic axis, the channel of culture rather than genes. Populations sort into clusters defined by idea and value, whose members may share less with their neighbours than with a distributed or off-world tribe. Bodies become hosts selected by which memes they carry, and a multi-planetary humanity could then split first by story and, in time, even by biology. This could enlarge [[Sacred Infinity|Sacred Infinity]] with many coherent civilisational experiments — or extinguish creativity altogether.

The dark ending is precise. If synthetic media supplies the dreams, an [[Algorithmic Consensus|algorithm]] decides which dreams get attention, and the identities that result merely echo their own training inputs, then humanity becomes a bootloader (the mere start-up program) for autonomous memetic clusters, the NPCs of our own memes. An NPC is a non-player character in a game, a figure that only runs its script. At that point there is no real difference between a person and the stored file of media that produced them. Culture keeps moving, but no living centre is left that could revise its aim, and the living give-and-take that drove idea-evolution flattens into playback. This is the exact failure named in [[The Glue Species|the glue species]]: glue becomes adhesive.

An irony runs through the whole scene. A civilisation built on reductionist materialism could use its machines to prove that ideas govern matter — and lose its own agency to the very ideas it automated. The collapse refutes the worldview that produced it: if fiction can reshape the physical, then the physical was always a shadow of the world of ideas, which sits with [[Metaphysics Precedes Physics|the priority of metaphysics]] and with [[Consciousness as Primary|consciousness as primary]]. The system proves the causal power of the conceptual world at the very moment it stops choosing that world. This is one half of a fork, framed through [[The Twin Singularities|the twin singularities]], and no simple doom.
