# Stratification and Cultural Complexity

## Overview

Historical cultural complexity often depended on surplus concentrated among elites with time to commission, preserve, and evaluate difficult work, while subordinated classes carried the material cost. The correlation creates a real tension but not a timeless law. Automation and distributed tools could democratise surplus, education, production, and evaluation; captured productivity could instead recreate the patron–serf structure at larger scale. The question is how to preserve demanding culture without requiring degraded lives as its substrate.
