# Impermanence and Signal

**Impermanence and Signal** — the reading of *anicca* (the Buddhist teaching that all things are impermanent): impermanence runs through the whole manifested world, yet patterns differ enormously in how long they last, which makes the growth of consciousness the art of sorting what deserves deep storage from spent cache.

Everything passes, but not everything passes at the same speed. That difference in speed is where the whole teaching begins. Think of the range. A mood may last minutes. A trauma pattern can last decades. A mathematical relation holds across civilisations. A [[The Logos|Logos]] (the organising intelligence behind a world) endures across whole universe-cycles. Impermanence is real along the entire scale, yet nowhere near uniform — so discernment is the task itself. Ordinary reality mixes noise and truth together, and the growth of consciousness is the endless work of sorting signal from noise: compressing a whole life down to the few bits that survive death. Those bits are what gets weighed at [[The Bardo Examination|the bardo examination]] (the single reckoning each life faces before rebirth).

Suffering enters as a storage error. Compare it to a phone. A healthy device clears its temporary cache — the scratch files it never needed to keep — while holding on to the photos you saved. *Duḥkha*, the Buddhist word for suffering, is the reverse: clutching at the cache, at what was always going to be flushed. Status, appearance, praise, possession, even one fixed moral vocabulary — all of it is scratch memory. Grasping such things as a permanent identity tries to lower the entropy (the disorder, the running-down) of what cannot be held still. The project must fail, and the friction of that failure is the suffering itself. There is a subtler trap: the wish to be rid of grasping is itself a grasping — the [[The Recursion of Desire|recursion of desire]] in miniature. Craving cannot be abolished by more craving. What loosens it is not a tighter grip but a changed relation to impermanence, one that stops demanding the unstable hold still.

This is also why the memory of an event can be more real than the event. The memory can be replayed by the mind, and its governing principles stand outside time, whereas the cause-and-effect that produced the event can never be rerun. Permanence belongs to the reproducible pattern — to [[Information as Record|the recording]] — not to the raw sensation recorded.

The teaching flags its own tension. "Everything is impermanent" can quietly become an excuse to preserve nothing. But some truths, skills, [[Promises and the Hierarchy of Freedom|promises]] and loving relationships are far more durable than the bodies now carrying them. Wisdom clings to no passing form, and discards no worthy inheritance.
