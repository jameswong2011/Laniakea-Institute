# The Three Attention Practices

**The Three Attention Practices** — the three operational exercises for separating awareness from the thought-stream: the train-station practice, the identity-classification practice, and the opposite-branch practice. Together they turn the theory of [[Sovereign Attention|sovereign attention]] into drill, each targeting a different link in the chain by which exogenous thought becomes worldview and action.

**The train-station practice.** Attention rests on the breath without forcing any special state. When a thought captures attention, the practitioner pictures having boarded a train; when the thought produces a tangent, another train has been boarded. On recognising the chain, attention returns to the breath — sitting at the station while trains arrive and depart. The point is not to resent the trains but to discover the station: a person who knows that a thought-stream is transport can decide whether to travel with it. It trains dis-identification — the discovery that identification with mental events is selective rather than compulsory.

**The identity-classification practice.** For a fixed interval, thoughts are recorded without editing for respectability, then classified as *like me* (readily accepted into identity), *not like me* (rejected, alien, shameful or opposed) or *neutral* (little identity charge). Recurring items from the first two categories are taken into contemplation: where did the classification come from, what emotion protects it, what social group does it signal, what would change if the label were reversed? The rejected category is the most informative — a mind often defines itself by what it excludes, and some exclusions are wise boundaries while others are projected capacities waiting for integration. It trains a map of the identity boundary itself.

**The opposite-branch practice.** Caught in a repetitive loop, the practitioner constructs the outcome opposite to the habitual prediction: predicted rejection becomes a plausible sequence of repair; fantasised rescue becomes the responsibilities rescue would require; condemnation of another becomes a reconstruction of the morality from which that person's act appears necessary — the seed of [[Understanding Opposed Dharmas|understanding opposed dharmas]]. Belief in the opposite is not required. The exercise restores the missing branch to [[Free Will and Decision-Space|decision-space]]; once both directions can be represented, the original thought is no longer destiny.
