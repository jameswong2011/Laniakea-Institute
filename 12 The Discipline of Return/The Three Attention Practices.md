# The Three Attention Practices

**The Three Attention Practices** — three hands-on exercises for separating awareness from the thought-stream: the train-station practice, the identity-classification practice, and the opposite-branch practice. Together they turn the theory of [[Sovereign Attention|sovereign attention]] into drill. Each one targets a different link in the chain by which a thought arriving from outside hardens into worldview and action.

These are simple drills, not lofty states. Where sovereign attention names the goal, these three exercises are its opening moves.

**The train-station practice.** Attention rests on the breath, without forcing any special state. When a thought captures your attention, picture yourself boarding a train. When that thought throws off a tangent, another train has been boarded. On noticing the chain, attention returns to the breath — you sit at the station while trains arrive and depart. The point is not to resent the trains but to find the station. Once you know a thought-stream is only transport, you can decide whether to travel with it. This trains dis-identification: the discovery that you can choose which mental events to call "me", rather than being dragged along by all of them.

**The identity-classification practice.** For a fixed interval, write your thoughts down without tidying them for respectability. Then sort each one as *like me* (readily let into your identity), *not like me* (rejected, alien, shameful or opposed) or *neutral* (little charge either way). Take the recurring items from the first two piles into reflection. Where did the label come from? What emotion guards it? Which social group does it signal? What would change if you flipped it? The rejected pile is the most revealing. A mind often defines itself by what it shuts out — and some of those exclusions are wise boundaries, while others are disowned capacities waiting to be reclaimed. This draws a map of the identity boundary itself.

**The opposite-branch practice.** When you are caught in a repeating loop, build the outcome opposite to your habitual prediction. Suppose you are certain a friend's silence means contempt; construct the equally plausible story in which they are simply overwhelmed. Predicted rejection becomes a believable path to repair. A fantasy of rescue becomes the responsibilities that rescue would demand. Condemnation of another becomes a reconstruction of the morality from which that person's act looks necessary — the seed of [[Understanding Opposed Dharmas|understanding opposed dharmas]]. You need not believe the opposite. The exercise simply restores the missing branch to [[Free Will and Decision-Space|decision-space]]. Once both directions can be pictured, the original thought is no longer destiny.
