# Understanding Opposed Dharmas

**Understanding Opposed Dharmas** — the discipline of treating most perceived evil as contact with another truth-and-morality system, and of rebuilding that system from the inside before passing judgement on it.

Most of what looks like evil is really a collision with a different moral world. A dharma here is a working code of truth and value — the rules by which a group decides what is real and what is right. One code prizes loyalty and another autonomy; one guards purity where another guards inclusion; hierarchy reads as care in one and domination in the next. A close-knit family treats keeping a secret as basic decency and meets a whistleblower who treats exposing it as basic honesty; each sees the other as plainly wrong. So the first duty on meeting apparent evil is to reconstruct the opposing [[Dharma-Law|dharma]] from within. An enemy you cannot make sense of cannot be integrated; it will only be fought as a caricature. The frame is cosmic: the universe is a soup of [[Dialectic|dialectical]] opposition — opposites held in productive tension — between mostly self-stable moral systems, and the friction between them generates the polarity that conscious activity runs on.

Understanding does not mean submission. Some systems stay stable only by pushing all their costs onto others, hiding the exits, and destroying rival ways of knowing. Their internal morality explains them without making them acceptable to the wider [[The Dharmakāya|dharmakāya]], the single living body they all belong to. One case stays in a class of its own: [[Yaldabaoth and Anti-Reality|anti-reality]], the pattern that attacks the very possibility of future relation, is the limiting case no reconstruction can redeem. This is not relativism, the claim that every view is equally valid. It is dialectical maturity: you cannot defeat what you refuse to inhabit.

Judgement then drops the snap verdict and proceeds through a series of questions. What value is this action trying to protect? Which beings count inside its moral circle? Where does it place its costs? Does it open or close future choice? Could its own rule survive being applied to the actor? What promise, consent or dependency changes the relation? Which larger body carries the result? The answers may not add up to one universal verdict. But they produce a more accurate entry in the ledger of [[Karma as Contextual Law|contextual karma]], the law that weighs an act by its jurisdiction and consults no fixed conscience.

At the scale of a single thought, the opposite-branch exercise among [[The Three Attention Practices|the three attention practices]] rehearses this discipline. At the scale of whole ecosystems, [[Wrathful Repair|wrathful repair]] governs the cases where understanding ends in justified opposition.
