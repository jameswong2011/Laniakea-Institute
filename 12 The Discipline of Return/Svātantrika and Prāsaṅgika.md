# Svātantrika and Prāsaṅgika

**Svātantrika and Prāsaṅgika** — two historical labels from Madhyamaka Buddhism, generalised into the twin movements of conceptual life: the source movement that constructs positions and the sink movement that dissolves them by consequence.

Svātantrika is the source. It constructs a positive position through explicit reasons and relatively autonomous arguments, birthing a form that can travel, be tested, and organise action. Prāsaṅgika is the sink. It follows a claim's consequences until its reifications collapse, dissects the form, and returns its energy to a wider field.

Historically, within the school descending from Nāgārjuna, the names mark detailed disputes over method — whether emptiness should be established through autonomous syllogisms or only by drawing out the unwanted consequences of an opponent's own commitments. Those debates are acknowledged, and only the topology deliberately retained: the pair generalise into a memetic technology that any tradition of thought can run.

Together they form a breath: construction → testing → consequence → dissection → emptiness → reconstruction. The source without the sink fills the world with unexamined systems; the sink without the source turns intelligence into permanent negation. Their circulation is qi moving through the pathways of [[Memetic Lifeforms|conceptual beings]] — source and sink for the life-force of symbolic energy. Ideas remain alive precisely because they can be both formed and dissolved.

In the larger architecture the pair is the engine of [[Conceptual Ascension|conceptual ascension]] and the formal skeleton of [[Dialectic|dialectic]]. Its emptying stroke is the operational use of [[Śūnyatā (Emptiness)|śūnyatā]]; its rebuilding stroke feeds [[Memetic Birthing|memetic birthing]]; and the whole cycle is compressed into the polarise-and-empty steps of [[The Daily Discipline|the daily discipline]].
