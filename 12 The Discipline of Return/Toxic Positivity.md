# Toxic Positivity

**Toxic Positivity** — spirituality that demands a pleasant interpretation as the price of belonging, so that seeing grief, anger, danger or injustice clearly becomes a moral failure.

This is a warning aimed at spiritual practice itself: practice, too, can become a farm. When belonging to a school or scene depends on reporting uplift, people quietly censor their own darker readings. The favoured story about reality is then protected by that self-censorship. The community's picture of the world degrades in exactly the places where pain was carrying real information. Law-of-attraction and manifestation teachings — the belief that focused positive thought draws matching outcomes — turn inward-looking at the point where another person's suffering is read as proof of their "wrong vibration", while the actual conditions causing that suffering are left untouched. A request to talk oneself and others into a positive mindset, against the evidence, is the corruption sitting at the base of manifestation practice.

The argument rests on reading [[Pain, Pleasure, and Change|pain]] as information. Pain is a signal: of damage, of broken trust, of incoherent action, of exhausted capacity, or of change that is needed. Denying the signal does not repair what it reports. It only breaks the system's ability to notice. Consider a smoke alarm: pulling out its battery because the beeping is unpleasant does not put out the fire. A positive outlook is genuinely useful when it widens what you can do. It becomes a kind of gaslighting — being pressured to distrust your own accurate perception — when it makes clear sight a moral failure.

The alternative on offer is not gloom. It is [[Sahā and Endurance|endurance]] — staying engaged with a reality you see clearly — and, at the scale of whole societies, [[Lucid Despair|lucid despair]]: keeping your moral compass while seeing [[The Farmed Planet|the enclosure]] without cosmetic filters. Face the dark, understand what it is doing, and act from informed agency rather than naïve cheer. This, not manufactured brightness, is what keeps [[Hope and Despair|hope]] from collapsing into fantasy.
