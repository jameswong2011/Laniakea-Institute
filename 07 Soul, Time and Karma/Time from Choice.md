# Time from Choice

**Time from Choice** — time is not the container minds act inside, but the exhaust of minds acting: no choice, no change; no change, no time.

Here is the ordinary view turned over. We picture time as a stage that exists first, with events acting on it. Reverse that. A choice means a change — one state before it, a different state after. That difference between before and after is what time actually is. So the dependence runs in a chain: without choice there is no change; without change there is no time; without time there is no experiencing mind. Read the chain backwards and the claim turns stark. A cosmos with no choosers would not be a slow or empty one. It would be a frozen block — a static informational structure with no before and after at all. Time is thus fundamental not to the universe, but to the experiencing of it.

This only works because mind comes first. If matter were primary, time would have to be presupposed as its stage. Because [[Consciousness as Primary|consciousness is primary]], time can instead be generated by decision. It is woven, like space, out of deeper relations rather than laid down in advance — the temporal companion to deriving [[Spacetime from Entanglement|space from entanglement]] on the physical side.

The generator is the first movement from unity into [[Polarity as Compression|polarity]] — the splitting of one into a play of opposites — and it takes two forms. Sequential change lets the field swap between states, one after another, and that yields time. Parallel change lets one part vary while another holds, and that yields separate worlds — the branching face of the same act, treated under [[The Entropic Branching Engine|the entropic branching engine]]. Together they build the grid on which finite minds can develop and [[Free Will and Decision-Space|choose]].

Human time is narrow. Space can often be surveyed and revisited; time runs one way. Memory gives partial access backward, anticipation only a symbolic reach forward, and the acting body is pinned to one moving frontier. This lopsidedness is the [[The 3.5-Dimensional Being|3.5-dimensional]] condition — half a dimension of temporal freedom rather than a whole one. A mind with fuller access might hold a lifetime as one standing shape, the way we take in a landscape at a glance, rather than as a vanishing string of moments.

Calendars make the narrow field governable. Day, month and year still track real celestial motion. But the seven-day week and the five-on, two-off working rhythm are institutional inventions. Repeated through wages, schooling and ritual, they come to feel like properties of time itself, the way a factory whistle can seem to carve up the day by nature. A more conscious civilisation would treat calendars as revisable interfaces, not laws. Whether time is finally present-centred, branching, or relational and retrocausal is left deliberately open — one of [[The Open Tensions|the open tensions]].
