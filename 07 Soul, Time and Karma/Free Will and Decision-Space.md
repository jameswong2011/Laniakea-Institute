# Free Will and Decision-Space

**Free Will and Decision-Space** — freedom as real but smaller than it feels: what an agent actually holds is a bounded decision-space, restricted by external and internal conditions, which growth can enlarge but never render unconditioned.

Most people perceive their freedom to be far greater than it is, yet every being remains "the ultimate Creator intrinsically". Positively defined, free will is the capacity to parse the underlying data structure of reality and make the computations that accumulate as [[The Soul Ledger|the soul]].

The restriction comes in two classes. **External constraints** include physical law, body, geography, money, social position, promises, institutions and the actions of other beings. **Internal constraints** include fear, trauma, addiction, ignorance, unstable attention, inherited values and habits that prevent an option from appearing thinkable. Two people under similar external conditions can therefore possess very different practical freedom.

Growth does not mean becoming unconditioned. It means enlarging the range of conditions that can be perceived, understood and answered rather than obeyed automatically: a stable mind sees more moves; a healed nervous system can remain present where an injured one must repeat an old defence (see [[Trauma as Compression Error]]); knowledge opens physical and conceptual routes; and ethical discipline prevents one agent's temporary expansion of freedom from destroying the future freedom of others — the same test [[Agency as the Measure]] applies to institutions.

The concept anchors the category. Each exercise of bounded freedom branches the world through [[The Entropic Branching Engine]]; [[Promises and the Hierarchy of Freedom|promises]] transfer portions of decision-space between beings; [[Karma as Contextual Law|karma]] audits the results across lives; and [[Sovereign Attention|sovereign attention]] is the precondition of having any decision-space at all.
