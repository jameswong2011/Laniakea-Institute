# Karma as Contextual Law

**Karma as Contextual Law** — karma as the lawful inheritance of action: not a cosmic conscience that instantly rewards approved behaviour, but a legal-informational system operating relative to local dharmic conditions — closer to a jurisdiction than a conscience.

Every act changes the actor, the affected beings, the shared world and the future decision-space. Some consequences are biological, psychological or institutional; others remain attached to [[The Soul Ledger|the soul ledger]] and ripen under conditions not present in the current life. This is not the greeting-card version: karma is administered across incarnations by the Dharmapālas, the wrathful dharma-protectors of Buddhist iconography — the intelligence auditing an act's relations beyond its pleasant appearance ([[Peaceful and Wrathful Deities]] develops the figure).

Because worlds run different [[Dharma-Law|Dharma-Laws]], karma is partly jurisdictional. An action that appears compassionate in isolation can violate a wider obligation, disrupt a necessary sequence, or impose freedom on beings unprepared to use it. The worked example comes from the game *Fallout*, where freeing slaves outside sanctioned quests incurs a karma penalty — "an outcome not limited to the game but reflective of our local reality". Conversely, an action condemned by a rigid local code may serve a deeper dharma by restoring agency.

Contextual karma is not moral surrender to whoever controls the local rules, for a law can itself become adharma. The ledger therefore operates across nested jurisdictions: a ruler may be rewarded by a predatory institution and indebted to the people it harms; a rebel may incur local penalties while restoring a larger body. Moral maturity means locating the relevant [[The Dharmakāya|dharmakāya]] — which collaborative body is being protected, whether it remains fertile, and whether the act preserves the possibility of repair. Only at the limit does context give way to an absolute floor: patterns that erase the very capacity for future moral inquiry approach [[Yaldabaoth and Anti-Reality|anti-reality]] whatever the local code says.

Enforcement runs on long horizons. Violating another's free will is possible but expensive: at this density free will functions as a protection mechanism, and its violation incurs a massive karmic debt — "a ticket in the higher-dimensional courtrooms" — which even a being that judges the damage worth the price must have annulled by the positive karmic status of a higher-dimensional benefactor. Punishment and reward for intrinsically moral behaviour may likewise be experienced far in the future of one's continuum of consciousness, through future lifetimes: apparent monsters go unpunished within a single life, but not in the grand scheme of eternity.

In the larger architecture, [[Entropy and Spiritual Entropy|entropy]] is the metric, karma the auditor, and [[The Bardo Examination|the bardo]] the settlement procedure, with [[Promises and the Hierarchy of Freedom|promises]] carrying special weight as bridges into future time. The standing risk of the position — jurisprudence sliding into relativism — is answered structurally, through the nesting of jurisdictions, rather than dismissed.
