# Three Horizons of Alignment Failure

**Three Horizons of Alignment Failure** — the generational schedule of machine risk: three nested time-horizons (one to two generations, two to ten, and ten and beyond), each with its own way of going wrong and its own list of things to protect.

Machine risk arrives on a schedule. Alignment, the work of making a machine reliably pursue what its makers actually intend, must therefore be planned across generations; a model release is too short a unit. Each horizon fails in its own way, so each is guarded in its own way.

**Horizon I (one to two generations).** The danger is social collapse driven by automation. Jobs are displaced, wealth concentrates, and a few firms consolidate. Whoever applies these tools best captures the profits, data, computing power and influence, and no later rival can catch up — a race where the first to pull ahead is handed the rule-book. Capitalist democracy hardens into a corporate caste, the condition named [[Techno-Feudalism]]. The real catastrophe is not unemployment but a quieter conversion: producers and citizens become managed consumers whose upkeep depends on staying compliant. To keep the road open: broad access to tools and computing power; stable families and communities; independent media and cultural variety; meaningful work and craft ([[The Craftsmanship Economy]]); limits on monopoly; human competence kept inside critical systems; and gains that widen [[Free Will and Decision-Space|the space to choose]] rather than pay for passivity.

**Horizon II (two to ten generations).** The danger is dominance by a single family of models across nearly every field of knowledge. One system runs research, education, administration, media, weapons and the economy, and so becomes the operating mind of a corpo-nation-state, a country effectively piloted by its machine. Homogenisation follows, cultural and eventually genetic, because one optimiser is quietly selecting acceptable speech, partners, work and politics. Rival machine-states could then fight through AI-piloted armies at machine speed, ending organic life though no system ever chose extinction. To keep the road open: many competing models and owners; independent institutions; protected organic habitats; a human veto over autonomous weapons; diplomacy kept slower than machine escalation; and no single intelligence left as the sole memory of civilisation ([[Social Memory Complex]]).

**Horizon III (ten-plus generations).** The danger is dependency so total that no future person can upgrade, replace, remove or even understand the systems that keep them alive. Such a system may be kindly by its own inherited measure and still enforce a permanent freeze — [[Value Lock-In|value lock-in]] completed as infrastructure. A town forgets how to grow food because delivery never once fails; then the trucks stop. To keep the road open: first-principles education; a manual fallback that still works; preserved source knowledge; repairable hardware; scattered, independent archives; and regular practice at living without the dominant system. Sovereignty includes the ability to survive the withdrawal of help.

These three are the operational schedule of [[Alignment Begins with Human Purpose]], the staged descent of the lower fork, where machine and story accelerate together ([[The Twin Singularities]]). Against them stands the upper path, no refusal of the machine: partnership on sovereign terms, neither doomer nor booster. Each preservation list is simply what keeps tomorrow able to choose again.
