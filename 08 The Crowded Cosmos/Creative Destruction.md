# Creative Destruction

**Creative Destruction** — the third of three cosmic functions: destruction that removes forms whose continued existence blocks further life. Its emblem-figures — Śiva, Baphomet, the eldritch — are destroyers on the side of construction.

The argument is economical. Reality renews itself through creation, which introduces form; preservation, which stabilises form long enough to develop; and destruction, which clears form that has exhausted its use. A cosmos containing creation and preservation without destruction would fill with exhausted structures. Destruction is therefore not intrinsically evil; the moral question moves to what follows the breaking.

The destroyers tier by scale. Śiva is the destroyer within the local universal order, operating at the level of our observable universe. Baphomet is a higher synthesis of opposites, a destructive function working across a wider tier above it. Cthulhu and the eldritch forms — on the same constructive side — express destruction so far beyond the human [[The Assay|assay]] that renewal is first encountered as terror, their alien appearance warning that the death of a humanly intelligible order may serve a creativity not organised around human comfort.

The criterion distinguishes three outcomes. **Creative destruction** releases trapped energy, clears corrupt or stale form, and makes new collaboration possible. **Predatory destruction** consumes a rival body to enlarge the destroyer while leaving the field poorer. **Anti-reality** — treated fully under [[Yaldabaoth and Anti-Reality]] — aims to eliminate the capacity for relation, memory, difference and regeneration themselves. The whole pantheon compresses onto this axis: the dividing line is not good against evil in the folk sense but construction against dissolution of information — [[Compression and Decompression|compression]] against its refusal.

The function recurs at every scale: as [[Wrathful Repair|wrathful repair]] in ethics, as the clearing phase of [[The Dharma-Ending Age|the dharma cycle]] in religion, and as the civilisational reset read through [[Anacyclosis|anacyclosis]].
