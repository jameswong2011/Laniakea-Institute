# Radical Syncretism

**Radical Syncretism** — the organising thesis of the crowded cosmos: Buddhism and the earlier Vedic religions, Gnosticism, Manichaeism and Zoroastrianism are one religion and one cosmology seen through different cultural lenses.

The claim rests on the shared six-step topology of [[One Sacred Conflict]]: unbounded ground, differentiation, capture, uncontained spark, path of knowledge, return. Where that structure recurs, what is present is not mutual borrowing but the same referent compressed through different cultural codecs — the differences belonging to the [[The Assay|assay]] of each civilisation rather than to the reality described.

The syncretic bridge is already built inside one of the traditions: Mani explicitly engineered his religion as a synthesis of the prophet-lines of Zoroaster, the Buddha and Jesus — a universal prophetic history that is plain scholarly record. The transmitted religions likewise show the same dialectical evolution of memesis: provisional teachings composed to correct a persistent misunderstanding, each later corrected in turn — [[Dialectic|dialectic]] operating on scripture itself.

The sharpest formulation is negative. If the historical Buddha or any advanced monk were alive today, he would not use the label "Buddhist", because the essence of dharma is the integration of all worldly wisdom available to the consciousness. Labels mark codecs, not referents; fidelity to one vocabulary is what the traditions at their heights warn against. [[Yeshua as Bridge|Yeshua's bridge]] and the modern translation of the Gnostic map into [[Simulation Theory as Neo-Gnosticism|computational vocabulary]] are continuations of the same integrative act.

The identity claimed is one of form and function rather than lineage: the systems are not historically or doctrinally identical — they are independent witnesses converging on one structure, which is how [[Trip Reports and Revelation|revelation]] is read in general.
