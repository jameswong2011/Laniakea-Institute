# Lived Abundance

**Lived Abundance** — the civilisational shift a mature world must make: from measuring reality by what it produces to what is actually experienced. Production is kept, but demoted to infrastructure. Flourishing is counted in lived moments that no optimiser — no system built to maximise a number — can fully see.

A reductionist economy — one that trusts only what it can measure — values whatever can be counted, sorted, multiplied and sold. So it pours out the convenient and the plentiful while missing the brief moments that make a life worth living: intimacy, wonder, beauty recognised, absorbed craft, humour, reconciliation, revelation, presence that will never come again. These moments are [[Qualia|qualia]] — the felt texture of experience, what a moment is actually like from the inside. Every attempt to pin them to a number shows [[Measurement as Transformation|measurement as transformation]]: the part that gets recorded becomes policy, and the part left out is quietly borne in private. An economy can count the meal on your plate; it cannot count the laughter around the table.

There is a physics beneath this. The good lives in the moment of change, not in the still state that follows — the way the thrill of a journey fades the instant you arrive. Such moments cannot be stockpiled; a civilisation can only keep making room for them. So the shift does not abolish production; it demotes it to infrastructure. Material plenty succeeds only when it frees time, attention, health and [[Agency as the Measure|agency]] for flourishing that can never be made fully legible to an optimiser — and when the economy protects those moments without crushing them into one more metric institutions learn to game.

The argument reaches across time. A society that expects rescue from outside — a cosmic [[Harvest|harvest]] — or its own near-term replacement simply extracts as hard as it can now. Waste, planned obsolescence, unrecycled material, fragile supply chains, neglected maintenance — these are the marks of a civilisation that has lost confidence in its own future. Real self-determination begins only when systems are built for descendants who may well reject today's preferences. That horizon is already written into agency, which counts "future cycles" among its terms.

The final test is planetary. It is not whether an order can predict the future, but whether it leaves future beings enough variation — biological, ecological, technical, memetic and spiritual — to choose a future at all. Civilisation is a womb in the full sense — the recovered meaning of [[Matrix as Womb|matrix]] — protecting growth without dictating the final shape of the life it carries. Its success is measured at birth: when the new consciousness can leave the container, remember what it learned, and create without having to rebuild the walls that formed it.
