# The Craftsmanship Economy

**The Craftsmanship Economy** — the proposal that material plenty should not make human hands unnecessary. People should make a real share of their own world. Status then shifts away from what you own toward the skill, care and originality of what you made or repaired yourself.

The argument starts with work itself. Work links your intention to a real consequence, and it lets your particular difference take visible form. When every object around you is an anonymous product of some distant factory, you live in a material world that carries none of your own [[Agency as the Measure|agency]], none of your own choosing or making. A craftsmanship economy would push people to make a meaningful share of their clothing, furniture, tools, food, art, home or digital surroundings. General trade skills would spread through the population, innovation would be democratised (many hands tinkering with an inherited design will improve it), repair would become something to be proud of, and possessions would carry the memory of who made them. A table you built yourself, and know how to mend, holds your history; a flat-pack you would simply throw away holds none.

This is not a rejection of advanced industry. Making semiconductors, medicine, transport and planetary infrastructure demands deep specialisation. Craftsmanship supplies the opposite and complementary pole: ordinary competence beneath those complex systems. A civilisation stays sovereign when enough of its people understand how their world is made to challenge the priorities of the few who make it at scale. This is the economic answer to the enclosure test of [[The Farmed Planet|the farmed planet]] (is this system growing people or extracting from them?), the counterweight to living as a tenant under [[Techno-Feudalism|techno-feudalism]], and the seed-keeping demanded by [[Anacyclosis|the civilisational cycle]]: a civilisation that remembers how to make can regenerate.

So open technical standards, accessible tools, repairable products, distributed energy, durable public archives and community workshops are not luxuries but spiritual infrastructure, the shared groundwork that keeps a people's inner life free. They stop creation becoming a remote service handed down by an unaccountable priesthood. And they train the restored creatorship of the passage [[From User to Magus|from user to magus]] — from someone who merely operates tools to someone who commands them.
