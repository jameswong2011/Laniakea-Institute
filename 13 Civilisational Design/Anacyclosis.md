# Anacyclosis

**Anacyclosis** — the cycle by which governments rise, decay and are reborn: monarchy gives way to tyranny, then to aristocracy, oligarchy, democracy, mob rule, and back to a single ruler. First mapped by the Greek historian Polybius, it is one register of a wider civilisational rhythm.

Every form of government carries a virtue and the seed of its own rot. A single ruler can coordinate fast, but inherited power stops answering to anyone and curdles into tyranny, as a firm built by a driven founder can pass to heirs who keep the title and lose the drive. The best then organise resistance and rule as an aristocracy. That circle closes around its own interest and becomes oligarchy, rule by a narrow few. The people reclaim [[Agency as the Measure|agency]] through democracy. Appetite and demagoguery scatter the common order into ochlocracy (mob rule) until exhaustion calls forth a new unifier and the wheel turns again.

The cycle is a warning, and its details are not fixed. A political form decays when its organising virtue comes apart from the lived feeling (the [[Qualia|qualia]]) that once animated it. A constitution cannot keep a people free if they no longer practise self-rule. An aristocracy is not "rule by the best" once its only test is who your parents were. And a democracy cannot stay common judgement when attention is privately engineered, the condition named [[Algorithmic Consensus|algorithmic consensus]]: a public square whose speakers are ranked by an unseen editor tuned for engagement.

The same rhythm runs beneath politics: breaking, then healing, then tranquillity, then rigidity, then breaking again. A larger break clears more ground and demands a longer healing. Healing can be deeply pleasurable as scattered parts find a new order. Tranquillity ripens that order, then hoards habits built for dangers long gone (a body still manufacturing antibodies for a plague that has vanished) until the weight of them forces renewal. The present turmoil belongs to this rhythm. The reset is a phase and no catastrophe, the breaking a chance for repair, tied to the scheduled institutional exhaustion of [[The Dharma-Ending Age|the Dharma-ending age]].

The lesson is emphatically not to maximise destruction but to carry seeds through the break: skills, truthful memory, local trust, spiritual practice, genetic and cultural variety, and the ability to rebuild essential systems without the failed centre. This is the work of [[Creative Destruction|creative destruction]], demolition with memory and craft. A civilisation that remembers only its monuments cannot regenerate. One that remembers how to make can.
