# Anacyclosis

**Anacyclosis** — the recurring cycle of political forms described by the Greek historian Polybius in the second century BCE, taken up as one register of a wider civilisational rhythm: monarchy → tyranny → aristocracy → oligarchy → democracy → ochlocracy → renewed monarchy.

Each form contains a virtue and the seed of its own corruption. Monarchy coordinates, but inherited power loses accountability and becomes tyranny. The best organise resistance and form aristocracy; the class closes around its own interest and becomes oligarchy. The people reclaim [[Agency as the Measure|agency]] through democracy; fragmented appetite and demagoguery dissolve the common order into mob rule, and exhaustion calls forth a new unifier.

The cycle is not destiny in every detail. It is a warning that political forms decay when their organising virtue separates from their lived [[Qualia|qualia]]: a constitution cannot preserve freedom if citizens no longer practise self-rule, an aristocracy is not "rule by the best" once selection measures only inheritance, and democracy cannot remain common judgement if attention is privately engineered — the condition analysed as [[Algorithmic Consensus|algorithmic consensus]].

The same rhythm appears beyond politics as breaking → healing → tranquillity → rigidity → breaking. Greater breakage creates greater opportunity for reorganisation and demands a longer healing; healing can be deeply pleasurable as scattered parts discover a new order; tranquillity ripens that order and then accumulates habits designed for dangers that no longer exist, until renewal begins because the body can no longer carry them. The present turmoil belongs to this rhythm: the reset is a phase rather than a catastrophe, the breaking period an opportunity for self-improvement, connected to the scheduled institutional exhaustion of [[The Dharma-Ending Age|the Dharma-ending age]].

The lesson drawn is emphatically not to maximise destruction but to preserve seeds through the break: skills, truthful memory, local trust, spiritual practice, genetic and cultural diversity, and the capacity to rebuild essential systems without the failed centre — the work of [[Creative Destruction|creative destruction]] rather than ruin. A civilisation that remembers only its monuments cannot regenerate; one that remembers how to make can.
