# The Anthropological Timeline of Human Ideas

**The Anthropological Timeline of Human Ideas** — the flagship proposal: a public, competitive map of how ideas and value-systems built the world we live in, ending in a shared effort to decide, deliberately, what a human being should be.

The modern mind is not one philosophy but many, stacked atop each other — ancient metaphysics, religious law, science, Enlightenment self-rule, nationalism, capitalism, socialism, colonial encounter, industrial discipline, mass media, computation and countless local traditions. Think of an old wall painted over many times: each coat hides and reshapes the one beneath. Every layer solved a problem, created a new one, and changed the meaning of the layers below. The Timeline traces these movements to show where a present intuition came from, and which rivals lost. Competing versions keep it honest, not one official history.

Take the Enlightenment: a correction to a corrupted church, restoring public reason and the right to question inherited authority. But when that correction hardened into the belief that matter is all there is — [[Science and Scientism|scientism]], the faith that measurement is the only road to truth — it mistook freedom from a captured church for freedom from the sacred. Agency grew; transcendence and durable community thinned; buying and selling became measurable stand-ins for meanings they could not replace. The next synthesis must keep the protection of inquiry while reopening the doors that reaction closed.

The work has six parts:

1. **idea genealogies** — the thinkers, cultures and crises through which a value changed;
2. **moral framework maps** — what each system treats as good, sacred, real and governable;
3. **consequence histories** — how each behaved once put into practice;
4. **translation layers** — where opponents ask the same question in incompatible words;
5. **suppressed branches** — serious alternatives that lost power without being disproven;
6. **future forks** — what each framework would keep or abandon under abundance and artificial intelligence.

The result is a **Democratic Project to Define the Human**. People study a menu of traceable moral structures, form their own positions, and gather into tribes without claiming to speak for everyone — the intake channel of [[Voluntary Moral Communities|voluntary moral communities]]. Decaying humanities are reborn as open competition among historians, philosophers, artists, contemplatives and machine-assisted teams, judged on breadth, coherence, accessibility, evidence and the power to state an opponent's case from inside it.

Planetary leadership should demand this capacity in public: power over many moral worlds belongs only to those who can recount how each formed, why intelligent people live in it, and what it fears losing. This is the essence of the human — to prune the old to make room for the new. Historical completeness and empathy become qualifications for [[Agency as the Measure|agency]], not decoration. The project matters more than any closed physical [[The Open Theory of Everything|theory of everything]]: physics explains the shared interface, but the Timeline exposes the goals of those about to redesign it. It is the highest pursuit of human agency, period. The chain is short: [[Alignment Begins with Human Purpose|aligning machines requires aligning ourselves]] first, which needs free persons choosing a moral stance. Against the machine that freezes morality by [[Value Lock-In|looking backward]], the Timeline keeps it alive by understanding how it was ever made.
