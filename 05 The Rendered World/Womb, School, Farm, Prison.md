# Womb, School, Farm, Prison

**Womb, School, Farm, Prison** — the taxonomy of functions a rendered world can serve, usually several at once: womb, school, laboratory, canvas, archive, farm and prison.

Seven functions are distinguished. A **womb**: a protected environment in which a new kind of consciousness develops. A **school**: a field of catalyst, feedback and moral choice. A **laboratory**: a bounded reality answering a specific qualia question. A **canvas**: a collaborative art-space for novelty. An **archive**: a way to preserve or replay patterns that would otherwise vanish. A **farm**: a system that extracts attention, labour, emotion or life force for another agent. A **prison**: a closed world whose inhabitants are denied accurate knowledge and meaningful exit.

The descriptions are not mutually exclusive, and that is the point. The same environment can be a school to one layer and a farm to another; the question is never *which is it?* but *which functions dominate, for whom, at which layer?* Two laws of degeneration govern the slide between functions: a womb becomes a prison when birth is indefinitely prevented, and a rule becomes oppression when it no longer serves the growth that justified it. The audit runs on the measures applied to every world — fullness, fidelity, agency, variation and exit ([[Simulacra and Fullness]]).

The taxonomy is the working instrument of the ethics of worlds. [[Matrix as Womb]] establishes that the womb function is primary and the prison reading a modern inversion; [[Entropic Preservation]] names the thermodynamic pressure that can slide efficient worlds toward the farm pole, awareness kept alive as harvestable sensation; [[The Farmed Planet]] applies the farm test to terrestrial institutions; and [[Ordered Closure]] governs the lawful ending of a world whose dominant function has expired. Which functions our own layer serves is held explicitly among [[The Open Tensions|the open tensions]] rather than answered by decree.
